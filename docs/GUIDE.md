## Before you start

**What you need.** A web browser and about ten minutes. Chrome, Safari, Firefox
and Edge all work. It works on a phone, but a laptop is easier for this kind of
task.

**What you do not need.** An account. An email address. A credit card. A download.
An AI subscription.

**Have your old resume nearby** in whatever form it exists: a PDF, a Word file, a
Google Doc you can copy the text out of, or even a rough list in your notes app.

**A word about the five tabs across the top.** They are numbered 1 to 5 and they
run left to right in the order you will use them. You can jump between them
freely at any time. Nothing is locked.

## Step 1. Your evidence

This is the raw material: everything you have done, listed one thing at a time.
It is not your resume. Think of it as your inventory. Most of it will never
appear on any single resume, and that is the point, because the tool picks the
right pieces per job.

### Getting your history in

You have four ways in. Pick whichever is least work.

**Paste my resume.** The fastest route. Drag your PDF or Word file onto the box,
or paste the text in. The tool reads the file inside your browser, cleans it up,
and splits it into separate entries. It tells you what it cleaned, for example
"smart quotes and dashes straightened" or "12 wrapped lines rejoined", so nothing
happens invisibly.

You can drop several files at once. Old resumes, a cover letter, notes. Every
entry from every file goes into the pile. Duplicates are skipped and you are told
how many.

**Clean up my resume first.** Choose this if your current resume has two columns,
text boxes, icons, tables or a sidebar. Those look fine to a human and get
shredded by the software companies use to read applications. This screen hands
you a prompt to paste into whichever AI you already pay for, along with your
resume. It reformats your own history into a shape that reads correctly, and it
is written to forbid inventing anything. Then you bring the clean version back
here.

**Add one by hand.** A blank form. Use this for anything that was never on a
resume.

**See an example.** Loads a fake person so you can see how everything behaves
before you put your own work in. Load it, click around, then clear it out with
the small ✕ on each entry.

### Grading each entry

This is the part that matters, and it is the whole product.

For each thing you did, the form asks **"Is there a number attached to this?"**
Your four options:

- **Not yet, or there is no number.** Completely acceptable. A line without a
  number is honest. A line with an invented number is a trap you set for
  yourself.
- **Yes, and I can show where it came from.** The strongest kind. You will be
  asked where the number came from, for example "Zendesk monthly export, Q2
  against Q1". This becomes **Proven**.
- **Yes, but it is my own estimate.** You worked it out yourself. You will be
  asked for your reasoning, so you can defend it out loud. For example "three
  people at about two hours each, measured against their own time logs". This
  becomes **My estimate**.
- **Only a volume count.** How much you did rather than what changed. "94 vendors
  onboarded". This becomes **Volume**.

There is also a field called **What stayed the same**. This is the most
underrated line on any resume. "Support tickets down 41%" is a claim. "Support
tickets down 41%, same headcount, same season" is evidence, because you have
removed the obvious alternative explanation.

### The quarantine

At the bottom of the form there is a dashed box for industry statistics.
Anything typed there is saved for your interview preparation and is **locked out
of your resume permanently**. It cannot leak onto the page.

The reason: if you write "the industry average handling time is six minutes" next
to your own work, every reader assumes six minutes was your result. That single
habit is where most accidental resume dishonesty comes from.

### The score on the right

**Resume strength** out of 100, plus a bar showing how your entries split across
the four grades, plus one line telling you the single next thing to do. You are
not aiming for 100. You are aiming for "as high as the truth allows".

## Step 2. The job you are going for

Two ways to fill this in.

### Find roles for me

Press **Search live boards**. Your browser calls the public job feeds directly,
reads somewhere around 13,000 to 15,000 live listings, and ranks them against
what is actually in your evidence. It takes under a minute.

Nothing about you is sent anywhere. The listings come to your device and the
matching happens here, which is why this can be free and private at the same
time.

Every filter starts at its widest setting on purpose, so your first run sees the
biggest possible pool. Narrow afterwards. If results come back thin, a panel
appears under them offering one click fixes, each of which widens exactly one
filter and runs the search again.

Each result shows:

- **Strong, Possible or Weak.** A first pass based on the job title and location.
- **A plain sentence** naming what overlapped, for example "Overlaps on marketing,
  growth marketing, demand generation and 2 more."
- **Where it came from and how old it is.** These are the board's own published
  timestamps, not a search engine's guess.
- **Pay**, when the posting states it. Hourly and monthly figures are converted to
  a yearly number so they can be compared.
- **new**, if it was not there last time you looked.

Then three buttons: **Open** the posting, **Tailor** it, which pulls the full
description and saves it as a job, or **Resume + CV**, which goes straight to the
tailored document.

Some job sites cannot be included. **Sources** explains exactly which and why, in
plain terms, one line each. LinkedIn and Indeed do not permit this kind of
reading. That is their rule, not a missing feature here, and the tool says so
rather than pretending.

### Add one myself

Paste the link to a posting and press **Fetch**. This works directly with
Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and SmartRecruiters, which between them carry a large
share of real postings.

If the link is from somewhere that blocks automated reading, open the posting,
select the description, copy it, and paste it into the big box instead. Same
result.

Press **Save this job**. The tool reads the posting and pulls out the terms it is
actually asking for. The employer's own name is deliberately excluded, because
you should not be marked down for not having previously worked at the company you
are applying to.

**You can save as many jobs as you like.** Each one keeps its own keywords and its
own selections, so two applications give you two different resumes from the same
inventory.

## Step 3. What earns a place on the page

Your entries, ranked by how much of this posting each one answers.

The number on the left of each row is that entry's fit out of 100. Green tags
show which of the posting's terms it hit. The switch on the right controls
whether it appears on the resume. Entries with a real overlap are switched on for
you; you can override any of it.

**Coverage** on the right is the headline number: how much of what the posting
asks for is answered by the entries you have switched on.

Underneath it, in red, is the list of things the posting names that your evidence
does not mention. This list is the most valuable thing on the screen, and you get
two ways to deal with it.

**Answer them myself** walks you through each one and asks a simple question: have
you done this? If yes, you describe it in your own words and it becomes a real
entry, so your score rises because your evidence rose. If no, it stays a gap and
gets reported as one. Nothing is written for you.

**Re-check with AI** needs a connected AI key and does something different.
Keyword matching is literal, so it calls something missing whenever you described
it in different words. A posting asks for "stakeholder management" and your
resume says "cross-collaborated across departments". Same thing, no shared words.
This reads what you actually wrote and sorts every gap into three buckets: what
you already said in other words, what only you can confirm, and what is genuinely
absent. The middle bucket asks you rather than assuming.

If a gap turns out to be real, that is worth knowing now instead of in the
interview.

## Step 4. Your resume

The document itself, live.

### Write it for this job

**Write my resume for this job** needs a connected AI key. It rewrites your own
entries into the posting's vocabulary, scores the result, and goes again on
whatever is still unmatched, up to three passes. It cannot change a fact, a date
or a number. Anything it cannot support from your entries goes on a list called
**Left out on purpose** rather than onto the page.

**Write the longer CV** does the same at CV length.

If it cannot reach 100%, it tells you the exact terms it fell short on and offers
**Show a 100% specimen**. That builds the resume of the person the posting was
actually written for, with every claim you cannot currently support wrapped in
[square brackets], and it cannot be exported. Its only job is to make the gap
concrete, and to tell you the smallest real thing you could do in the next month
to close each one.

Without an AI key you still get a full resume, assembled from your entries in
order of relevance to the posting. That is the original behaviour and it is
complete.

### Everything else on this screen

- **Save as PDF** opens your browser's print window. Choose "Save as PDF" as the
  destination. The file is named after you and the job, so twenty applications do
  not produce twenty identically named files.
- **Edit heading** is where your name, contact details and summary live. Fill this
  in early. A resume that exports saying "Your Name" is the most expensive
  mistake this tool could let through, so it warns you.
- **Formatted** and **Edit as text** switch between the laid out document and a
  plain text version you can edit freely. Your hand edits win and are kept until
  you press Discard.

### The ATS check

**ATS** stands for applicant tracking system. It is the software almost every
employer uses to read and file applications. If it cannot read your file
properly, a human may never see it.

The score runs fourteen structural checks against the document actually on your
screen, not against a template, and every check is listed so you can see exactly
what passed. Things like: no tables, single column, real text rather than
pictures of text, standard bullet characters, plainly named sections, and no
rules or filled bands that a parser can mistake for a shape.

Aim for 100. If it drops, the failing line names the reason.

Honest limit: no tool can promise a particular third party checker will return
100, because several of them score keyword overlap against a specific job, which
depends on your content rather than the template. What is checkable here is the
structure.

**Typeface** lets you change font, size, spacing and colour. Every font offered is
a real text font that parsers read reliably. Decorative and icon fonts are not
offered at all, because they are the most common reason a resume scores badly
before a human ever sees it.

One thing to know: choosing **Monochrome, safest** adds a thin line under each
section heading, and the checker counts any visible line as a graphic, so the
score drops by seven. That is the checker being consistent rather than a fault.
If you want the maximum score, keep **Accent headings**.

## Step 5. Send it, and track it

The work does not end when the document looks right.

**Ready to send** is a checklist for the job you have open. Name and contact
details present, enough entries selected, enough of them carrying a number,
coverage above a sensible floor, ATS structure checked, screener answers filled.
Every failing line has a "take me there" link.

**Things you write once** gives you three pieces of writing, assembled from your
graded evidence and nothing else: a short "why I fit" paragraph, a cover letter,
and a note to send a person directly. No model invents a reason, because a reason
it invents is a reason you cannot defend.

**Your applications** is the tracker. Set the status yourself. Nothing is sent or
checked on your behalf, ever.

**Answer bank** holds the twelve questions every application form asks. Work
authorisation, sponsorship, salary expectations, notice period, portfolio link,
and so on. Fill them in once, then copy each one in a click, forever.

**The follow up** is the part that quietly produces most interviews and the part
almost everybody drops. Six days after you mark something as applied, a banner
appears offering to draft a short note. Two sentences is enough.

## Optional: connecting your own AI

**You do not need this.** Everything described above works without it. Skip this
section unless you want the four AI features.

### What it costs

You pay your AI provider directly for what you use. There is no markup, no
subscription here and no middleman, because your browser talks to them directly.

Tailoring one resume and CV is a small request. On a cheap model it is fractions
of a cent. On the most expensive models it is still cents rather than dollars.
Several providers have a free tier that covers this comfortably.

### The easiest way

Press **Connect AI** at the top right, then **Sign in with OpenRouter**. You
approve it on their site and land back here connected. One connection reaches
Claude, GPT, Gemini, Llama, DeepSeek, Mistral, Grok and Qwen.

### Or paste a key

A "key" is a long password that lets a program use your AI account. Press
**Connect AI**, pick your provider, follow the link to their key page, copy the
key, paste it in.

You do not need to know which provider your key belongs to. Paste it and the tool
recognises the shape and sets everything up.

### Is that safe

The key is stored in your browser only, and it is sent only to the provider you
chose. There is no server in this product for it to travel through. Press
**Disconnect** at any time and it is gone from the device.

Two sensible habits, the same ones any developer would use: set a spending limit
on the key in your provider's dashboard, and do not paste a key into a shared or
public computer.

### If it does not work

Press **Run full diagnostic**. It tests each layer in order and names the one that
broke: no key, cannot reach the provider, key rejected, model no longer exists,
or the request itself failed. "Check your key" is useless advice when the key is
fine and the model was retired last month, so it does not say that.

## Keeping your work safe

Everything lives in your browser on this device. Nothing is uploaded. That means
nobody can see your resume, and it also means nothing follows you to another
computer on its own.

**Automatic snapshots.** A snapshot is kept every couple of minutes as you work
and the last five are held. Step 5, under **Backups**, lists them by time and
size. **Restore** rolls back to any of them.

**Do this once, now.** Step 5, **Download everything**. That saves a single file
holding all of it: entries, jobs, answers, tracker, settings. Keep it somewhere
you trust.

**Moving to a new computer.** Take that file across, then use **Open saved file**
on step 1.

**What will lose your work.** Clearing browsing data or site data for this
address. Using "private" or "incognito" mode, which forgets everything when the
window closes. Switching browsers or devices without carrying the file across.

## Installing it like an app

You can put it on your home screen or dock so it opens like any other app. It
still runs entirely on your device.

- **iPhone or iPad.** Open the link in Safari, press the Share button, then **Add
  to Home Screen**.
- **Android.** Open the link in Chrome, press the three dot menu, then **Add to
  Home screen**.
- **Mac or Windows.** Open the link in Chrome or Edge, then look for the install
  icon at the right of the address bar, or the three dot menu, then **Cast, save
  and share**, then **Install page as app**.

## When something looks wrong

**Only one or two entries came out of my resume.**
Your file's layout is fighting the parser, which means an applicant tracking
system will struggle with it too. Use **Clean up my resume first** on step 1. That
is the real fix and it makes every future application easier.

**My PDF would not read at all.**
It is probably a scan, which is a picture of a document rather than text. No
browser can read those. Copy the text from wherever the original lives, or run it
through the clean up prompt with your own AI.

**A job link would not fetch.**
Some sites do not permit automated reading. Open the posting, copy the
description, paste it into the big box on **Add one myself**. Same outcome.

**The search found almost nothing.**
Look at the panel under the results. It names the exact filter costing you volume
and offers a one click fix. If every filter is already at its widest, the pool for
that search is genuinely small.

**A number I wrote is not showing on the resume.**
Check the entry is switched on in step 3, and check the number went in the
number field rather than the quarantine box at the bottom, which is locked out of
the document by design.

**Everything vanished.**
Step 5, **Backups**, then **Restore** the most recent snapshot. If the browser
data was cleared entirely, use **Open saved file** with your downloaded copy.

**Something else.**
Open an issue at
[github.com/joshuajackai/evidence-engine/issues](https://github.com/joshuajackai/evidence-engine/issues).
Describe what you expected and what happened. Please do not paste your resume
into a public issue.
